THE DESIGN INTENT

A house for this family,
and this climate.

01

Family & privacy

Grandparents live on the ground floor. Upstairs: a parents’ suite, three children’s rooms and a flexible guest bedroom. Two staff bedrooms accommodate three people beside a basement lightwell. Guest reception is close to the front door; family living opens onto a private pool court.

02

Build for Peshawar

A proposed reinforced-concrete frame with brick infill, insulated and waterproofed flat roofs, protected wall insulation, light exterior finishes, and recessed low-e glazing. Projecting chajjas and vertical screens temper the west-facing front. Kitchens, baths and services are grouped to shorten runs.

03

Water & the pool

The 8 × 20 ft pool sits inside the buildable area, away from the basement footprint. A cover reduces evaporation. Filtration, changing facilities and a holding tank are reserved. Roof rainwater storage is separate from drinking-water storage. Pool discharge and basement pumping need a detailed services design.

04

Energy before equipment

Shading, roof/wall insulation, ceiling fans, zoned inverter cooling and solar hot water reduce demand before adding 26 PV modules and battery storage. Daytime pool filtration follows solar production. Batteries store energy; they cannot correct a persistent generation deficit.

Decisions still to confirm

West-facing road is provisional. DHA Peshawar is the assumed authority. Setbacks used: 15 ft front, 7 ft rear, 5 ft sides; a published older amendment allows 5 ft rear, so this concept keeps the more conservative 7 ft. Plot survey, soil/groundwater, neighbouring shadows, final floor heights, local approvals and the energy budget remain open.

Concept stair flights are 4 ft wide with 11 in treads; floor levels are −8′6″, +2′, +14′ and +26′. This is an architectural visualization, not an engineered or approved construction design. Concrete member sizes, reinforcement, seismic resistance, retaining walls, waterproofing, ventilation, battery protection and pool structure must be designed by qualified local professionals.

Furniture is visual only and does not block walking. Day/evening light illustrates mood, not a daylight or solar-shadow analysis.

Full off-grid operation has not been established. Use the energy panel to see the effect of cooling demand. Continuous supply through poor-weather periods may require lower loads, an approved larger solar resource, or an independent backup source.

Interior direction

Warm ivory walls and pale stone connect walnut furniture, linen upholstery, muted olive, clay and slate-blue accents. Bedrooms have individual palettes; staff rooms receive complete furnishings and the same material direction. Use Interiors for each room’s finish schedule and furniture list.
